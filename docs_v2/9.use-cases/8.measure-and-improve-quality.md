# Measure and improve AI quality

Changing a prompt or swapping a model feels like progress, but without measurement it is a guess. This page explains why AI applications need evaluation to improve reliably, how DIAL supports an eval-driven build loop, and where the limits of automated measurement lie. It is for teams deciding to take quality seriously and for the developers who would instrument it.

## Why this matters

AI output is not deterministic, so the usual signals of software quality do not apply. A change that makes one answer better can quietly make ten others worse, and "it looks good in the demo" is the most expensive sentence in an AI project. Teams that tune by intuition end up trading one failure for another without knowing it, because they never measured the whole.

Evaluation replaces intuition with evidence. Define what good looks like, score changes against it, and a prompt edit or model swap becomes a measured improvement or a measured regression rather than a hopeful one. This is what lets a RAG application or an agent get steadily better instead of merely different with each release.

## What you can do with DIAL

The Evaluation section of [DIAL Admin](../5.administering-dial-new/4.evaluation/0.index.md) measures models, applications, and MCP tools against test cases you define. Metrics score accuracy, answer relevance, faithfulness to retrieved context, or custom criteria, and ready-made metrics score the retrieval and answers of RAG applications. Metrics that use a model as a judge call a model deployed in DIAL Core.

The point is the loop, not a single score. Establish a baseline, make a change — a different retriever setting, a revised prompt, another model — and re-run the test suite to see whether the change helped, hurt, or did nothing. DIAL Admin compares two runs side by side and follows score trends, and `eval-cli` runs test suites from a pipeline, so the loop can run on every change.

## How you'd build it

- **Understand evaluation in DIAL.** [Evaluations](../2.understand-dial/3.capabilities/9.evaluations.md) frames what DIAL can measure and how a run works.
- **Enable it.** [Configure the evaluation services](../6.services/10.evaluations/1.configuration/0.index.md) adds the two services behind the Evaluation section of DIAL Admin.
- **Run the loop.** [Evaluation in DIAL Admin](../5.administering-dial-new/4.evaluation/0.index.md) covers datasets, test suites, and runs, and [Configure a test suite](../5.administering-dial-new/4.evaluation/1.test-suites.md) picks the deployment and scoring metrics.
- **Pair it with a RAG build.** [Chat with your knowledge base](2.chat-with-your-knowledge-base.md) is the scenario this loop most often improves.

## Why DIAL over the alternatives

Eyeballing outputs in a spreadsheet does not scale and does not catch regressions you did not think to look for. General-purpose evaluation frameworks exist, but they run beside the platform. DIAL runs evaluations where the models and applications already live: test requests and judge calls pass through DIAL Core under the same access rules and limits, and results include cost and timing. Its honest limit is the limit of all automated evaluation: a metric is a proxy for quality, not quality itself, and an LLM judge has its own biases. Evaluation narrows the space where human review is needed; it does not remove the need for judgment on what to measure.
